## The job of a track in this form

**Getting in the zone** — background for a gameplay session. It holds a mood without demanding you
follow it. You disappear *into* it; it never tugs at your focus. (This is form-level: the music's
purpose shapes the form.)

## The core shape (one line)

A **fixed, driving floor** — locked pulse, subdividing tighter, heavy percussion building, bass-rich
and deep — with a **dramatic operatic swell riding over the top**. The rhythm keeps you *locked*
(the zone); the drama keeps you *in* it. **Steady floor, epic ceiling.**

## Rhythm — the driving floor

- **Fixed time signature.** A locked, steady pulse underneath — entraining, binaural-adjacent
  steadiness. The floor never wanders.
- **Drive by fractional subdivision, not by tempo.** The pulse subdivides *into itself* — `4 · 4 · 4 · 4
  → 2-2 · 2-2 → 1-1-1-1` — the beat fracturing tighter, rhythmic density climbing while the signature
  stays locked. "Fractional degrades. Double beats." Intensity comes from the beat *fracturing*, not
  from speeding up.
- **Heavy percussion is the build engine.** Percussion carries the climb. Deep, driving.
- **★ Drive, never chaos.** The locked floor is what makes intensity *cohere*: even when a build runs
  fast or frantic, the fixed pulse holds it as **drive** — directional, controlled — not chaos.
  (Battlewrath: "there is a difference between chaos and drive.") **This is the governing line for the
  whole form** — franticness is licensed only while the floor keeps it driven.

## Register — earned drama

- **Operatic. Dramatic. Powerful** — but the drama is *earned*, never "intense for the sake of it."
  Grand and grim.
- **Dynamic contrast is mandatory.** **Soft, desperate moments** — but as *contrast within the drive*,
  never a whole whispery/slow track. Restraint → release.

## Dynamic shape — pause · bellow · build

The emotional arc is the payload. Swells that **rise and recede**: pause, bellow, build. Both a
**long-form journey** (a ten-minute climb–peak–recede–reclimb you vanish into, à la *Ghost Love Score*)
and a **tighter driving pocket** (a locked groove that holds you) are in-bounds — a mix to experiment
with, not one fixed length.

**Tempo is both** — slow and fast both belong. **Frantic is permitted as a _builder_** — the fast climb
into a peak — but never as the resting state, and never as chaos: the fixed floor keeps a frantic build
*driven* (see Rhythm's governing line). Baseline sits slow-to-mid; franticness is a tool deployed for the
build, then released.

## Instrumentation — IN

- **Grand organ** — takes the "wall" space where a guitar would sit, and is the **primary keyed
  weight** (preferred over piano for exactly that heft). Gothic, filling; its weight comes from the low
  end and the swell, not from distortion.
- **Low strings** — cello, and the *low register* of the violin family. The dramatic melodic voice, but
  **weighted low** — dark and bodied, never shrill up top. (Violin: yes — low, not classical, not
  atmospheric.)
- **Heavy percussion** — the build engine again; deep and driving.
- **Bass** — rich, deep low-end foundation. The whole thing sits low.
- **Choir** — massed voices for the "swirling depth."
- **Gothic chant** — used as **breaks**: the drop/interlude that resets before the swell climbs back.
  Often wordless or Latin.
- **Church bells** (resonant, deep-toned — **not tinny bells**) — in the **hi-hat space**: the
  high-frequency rhythmic element up top, striking where a hi-hat normally marks the subdivisions.
  Gothic weight instead of a thin tick. _(Generation caveat below — wanted, hard to get.)_
- **Harpsichord** — welcome; its bright baroque attack suits the victoriandustrial texture.
- **Piano — minimal, background only.** Liked *less* than the organ; it has a place low in the mix, never
  up front. _(Generation caveat below.)_

## Instrumentation — OUT (hard lines)

- **No guitar. At all.** Organ and low strings fill that space instead. (This is an identity, not a
  subtraction.)
- **No synth-focus.** Darkwave, yes — but not over-synthed. Organic bones.
- **Never tinny / bright-brittle.** Bass-rich over tinny, always. Weight and body; no thin top.
- **Not "classical"** (concert-hall / chamber) and **not "atmospheric"** (ambient-pad drift). The fixed
  percussive floor and the pulse keep it out of both.

## Vocal architecture

- **Lead — a big voice moving slow.** Powerful, operatic, **unhurried**; swelling, trailing off into
  depth (Tarja-like). Slow with *mass* — the opposite of a whispery-slow track.
- **Around the lead — whisper-tones**, woven *around* the strong voice as texture (this is where "soft &
  desperate" lives — texture, not the whole track).
- **Under it — gothic choir** for the swirling depth.
- **Breaks — gothic chanting.**
- **★ Voice-as-instrument is primary.** The voice is **a motion of emotion and soul, not a recounting of
  events.** Lyrics *exist* — directional, story-shaped — but their job is the **cadence**: the pause, the
  bellow, the build. **You ride the delivery; you don't follow the plot.** (Hard line.)

## Sonic character

Bassy, rich, deep, warm. Weight and body throughout; no tinny top. The mix lives in the low end.

## Reference anchors (the sourced exemplars)

- **Nightwish — _Ghost Love Score_ · _The Phantom of the Opera_** — operatic power, slow swell, swirling
  depth, the long-form build.
- **Emilie Autumn — _Opheliac_** — victoriandustrial darkwave; violin, harpsichord; desperate-operatic;
  cadence over recounting.
- **Mishkin Fitzgerald — _Incitatus_** — dark cabaret; piano; theatrical, earned drama.

## The bridge to a prompt (light, forward-looking)

When a track gets built, **this form supplies the *style / production / structure* half** of a Suno
prompt (the instrumentation, the pulse, the vocal architecture, the arc). **Content supplies the other
half** — the theme and any lyrics — attached separately from an identity's mood. Form × content = a
prompt. _(The content-attachment layer isn't built yet; this is only the pointer.)_

## Generation caveats (targets for the prompt library)

Wanted, but Suno tends to fumble these — flagged so the prompt library goes at them deliberately, not by
accident:

- **Church bells in the hi-hat space** — hard to get generating cleanly; wanted badly. A craft target.
- **Piano** — "loses some richness or direction" when generated; part of why it stays background, not a
  lead. Lean on the grand organ for keyed weight instead.
